| Small firm (5–50 active cases) | **Top-3 requirement** | Manual data entry becomes bottleneck |

### Case Management Adoption

- **65–75%** of PI firms use case management software
- Leading PI platforms: Clio Manage, Filevine, CASEpeer, SmartAdvocate, Needles, Litify, Smokeball
- PI adoption is higher than general practice because PI is document-intensive and deadline-driven

### ROI / Reporting Awareness

- **Only 20–30%** of solo PI firms actively track cost-per-signed-case or channel-level ROI with rigor
- The majority evaluate performance by "are we busy enough?" — a gut-feel metric
- Most firms track only 1–2 lagging indicators (signed cases, cost per new case) and zero leading indicators (speed to contact, contact rate, qualification rate)

**Implication:** TrueVow's reporting should be dead simple: "Here's how many calls came in. Here's how many became consults. Here's your cost per consult." Don't over-engineer — these buyers don't use complex analytics.

### Format Breakdown (PI, Solo/Small)

| Format | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Phone consultations** | **50–65%** | Dominant for initial PI screening |

### Typical PI Consultation Duration

- Initial phone qualification: 5–15 min (done by intake staff or attorney)
- Substantive consultation: **30–60 min** (standard). Free. 60 min for in-person; 30 min for phone.

### Consults Per Month (Solo PI)

- Typical solo: **5–15 new consults/week** (20–60/month)
- At $5,000/mo ad spend: **12–20 qualified consults/month** (from 30–50 calls)

### TrueVow Impact: Additional Attorney Hours

If TrueVow books 6–10 *additional* consults/month:
- Additional consult time: 6–10 × 30–60 min = **3–10 hours/month**
- Plus prep/follow-up: ~30 min per consult = **3–5 hours/month**
- **Total: ~6–15 additional attorney hours/month**

At $250–$350/hr opportunity cost = $1,500–$5,250 in attorney time. Offset by value of new signed cases (~$10,230 in fees per case at avg $31K settlement × 33%).

## SECTION J: THE PSYCHOLOGICAL SELF-SELL FORMULA

### The Problem with "Value Differentiation"

The 40–55% of solos who self-answer are **not comparing TrueVow to Smith.ai.** They are comparing TrueVow to "just keep answering the phone myself" — which is free. No amount of "deterministic intake logic" or "A+ qualification algorithms" converts this cohort. They don't speak that language. 59% don't budget for technology at all.

### What Actually Makes a Self-Sell

| Trigger | Why It Works | ICP Data Point |
|---|---|---|
| **"You're losing $1,900/mo in ad spend to voicemail"** | Concrete, calculable, immediately verifiable. Their own number, not ours. | 38% voicemail abandonment × $5,000/mo ad spend = $1,900/mo wasted. |
| **"$499/mo vs. $4,000/mo for a receptionist"** | They know what a receptionist costs. The anchor is real in their financial world. | Receptionist TCO: $48,000/yr ($4,000/mo). Documented. |
| **"No per-lead billing. No per-minute billing. One price."** | Directly addresses fear #2 (unpredictable billing). Anti-Smith.ai positioning: Smith.ai charges $11.50/call over 30. TrueVow: one flat price. | Smith.ai per-call pricing publicly visible. TrueVow flat = differentiation. |
| **"Try it. Hear Benjamin answer a live PI call. 8 minutes."** | They don't need to understand AI. They need to hear it work. Demo > explain. | Behavioral: "show, don't tell" converts higher than feature lists. |
| **"You take a weekend. Benjamin answers."** | Not a feature — an emotional outcome. Addresses burnout directly. | 28% depression, 19% anxiety in legal profession. 50–60+ hour weeks. Always on call. |

### The Self-Sell Page Flow

1. **Pain:** "38% of your ad spend goes to voicemail. That's $1,900/mo." (Make them feel the leak.)
2. **Demo:** "Hear Benjamin answer a PI call — 8 minutes." (Show, don't tell.)
3. **Price:** "One price. All records visible. No per-lead billing. $499/mo." (No jargon.)
4. **Outcome:** "You take a weekend. Benjamin answers." (Emotional close.)

**Nowhere on the page:** "deterministic logic," "AI-powered," "machine learning," "A+ scoring engine," "unlock methodology."

### C. THREE USE CASES — HOW ATTORNEYS ACTUALLY USE LEVERAGE

Each use case references real API endpoints and features that exist in `TrueVow_Tenant_LEVERAGE_Service`.

#### Use Case 1: The SOL You Almost Missed

**What happens:** Attorney has 18 active PI cases. One slip-and-fall from January 2024 has a statute of limitations in 14 days. The case hasn't been touched in months because the client was slow providing medical records.

**What LEVERAGE does:**

1. Attorney calls `POST /deadlines/sol` when opening the case — LEVERAGE looks up the state's SOL rule from `leverage.validation_rules`, computes the filing deadline, and stores it via `POST /deadlines/case/{id}/save`.

2. Attorney subscribes to notifications via `POST /leverage/notifications/subscribe` with event type `deadline.critical`. LEVERAGE fires a webhook when a deadline enters the critical zone (1-30 days).

3. Attorney calls `GET /deadlines/upcoming?days=30` every Monday morning. Returns: *"Smith v. Walmart: SOL in 14 days. Urgency: CRITICAL. Estimated case value: $45K-$65K."*

**The aha moment:** The SOL countdown is red. The case hasn't had a demand letter drafted yet. LEVERAGE caught it with 14 days to spare — enough time to draft a demand letter, file a complaint, or at minimum toll the SOL. The attorney just avoided a malpractice exposure on a $50K+ case.

**Hook:** This attorney now opens LEVERAGE every Monday morning like checking email. `GET /deadlines/upcoming` is the first API call of the week. It's a habit — not a feature they evaluate.

#### Use Case 2: The Demand Letter That Asked for Too Little

**What happens:** Attorney prepares a demand letter for a rear-end collision case. They type "$45,000" based on gut feel — $22K medicals + $8.4K lost wages + some pain & suffering.

**What LEVERAGE does:**

1. Attorney calls `POST /leverage/damages` with structured inputs: medical bills ($22K), lost wages ($8.4K), pain & suffering multiplier (2x the medicals). LEVERAGE computes: economic damages = $30.4K, non-economic = $44K, gross damages = $74.4K. Settlement range: $44.6K-$63.2K (60%-85% of gross).

2. Attorney saves the worksheet via `POST /leverage/case/{id}/damages/save`. The worksheet is versioned and append-only.

3. Attorney calls `POST /leverage/case/{id}/compliance`. LEVERAGE checks: medical summary present? Yes. Injury description present? Yes. Liability statement present? No — returns `DEMAND_LETTER_INCOMPLETE` flag.

**The aha moment:** The attorney originally planned to demand $45,000. LEVERAGE's structured estimate is $74,400 — a $29,400 difference. The demand letter is also missing a liability statement. The attorney revises the demand to $72,000 and adds the liability section. The insurance adjuster counters at $58,000 and they settle at $64,000 — $19,000 more than the original $45,000 gut-feel number.

**Hook:** The attorney paid $99 for LEVERAGE this month and potentially added $19,000 to a single settlement. They will run `POST /leverage/damages` on every demand letter from now on.

#### Use Case 3: The Settlement Decision With Data

**What happens:** Insurance adjuster offers $38,000 on a case. The attorney thinks it's low but isn't sure by how much. The client wants to settle. Three months of negotiation fatigue is setting in.

**What LEVERAGE does:**

1. Attorney calls `POST /leverage/valuation/calculate` with the case state (Texas), saved damages, and settlement offer ($38,000). LEVERAGE applies the Texas multiplier config, adjusts for comparative fault (if applicable), and computes a projected after-contingency range.

2. Attorney calls `GET /leverage/case/nudge-pending` to see if this case is past its estimated settlement window.

3. Attorney calls `POST /leverage/case/{id}/settle` to record the outcome, then `PUT /leverage/case/{id}/settlement-details` to capture the full negotiation history (demand, offer, counter amounts, rounds, final amount, fault %, consent for intelligence network).

**The aha moment:** LEVERAGE shows a projected settlement range based on comparable cases in the same jurisdiction. The adjuster's $38K offer is below the low end. The attorney counters at $62K with data — not with "I feel like it should be more." They're negotiating with structure, not emotion.

**Hook:** That one valuation helped the attorney counter with confidence. They'll run `POST /leverage/valuation/calculate` before accepting any settlement offer going forward. The `settlement-details` capture feeds the SETTLE intelligence network, making future valuations more accurate for every attorney on the platform.

### AK-6. Pain of Loss — The Habit Flip

The user's requirement: *if the product were taken away, they should feel the pain.* The mechanism is the **Habit Flip** — the same Habit force that blocks adoption (9/10, Section AB) becomes the retention moat once adoption happens. Four design levers:

| Lever | Mechanism | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The Leak Report makes the alternative visible** | Month-1 report quantifies what voicemail would have cost them. Removal now has a visible price tag | T+30d, then monthly |
| **2. The first weekend off** | The summer "take the weekend" push lets them *experience* life without the phone. Losing Benjamin = re-buying the phone. Loss aversion on lived experience beats any marketing claim | T+60d, seasonally timed |
| **3. Accumulated assets** | TRACE readiness boards, Nurture sequences, intake records — assets that vanish on cancellation. This is the switching-cost power from Section AC, built deliberately through the Day-1 path | T+60d onward |
| **4. Churn-risk instrumentation** | Track pain-of-loss *before* it happens: days since last A+ text, days since last call, attached services count, logins. A firm with 14+ days of silence gets the removal-preview message: "If you pause Benjamin, your 38% voicemail rate returns. Want to see this month's leak estimate instead?" | Always-on |

**The flip sequence:** Weeks 1–2 the attorney thinks "is this worth $499?" → Week 2 the first A+ dinner text lands → Week 4 the Leak Report → Week 8 the weekend → Month 3+ they think "what would I do without it?" The Forces of Progress Habit score flips sides at roughly Day 60. Retention strategy = accelerate everything that moves the flip earlier.

### AK-7. Forces of Progress Re-Run — Under the SLIP Design

The redesign does not change Push (the market's pain) — it changes Anxiety and Habit (the two forces that block the switch). Re-running Section AB with the SLIP experience applied:

**Persona A:**

| Force | Before (Section AB) | After SLIP Design | What Changed It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push | 8 | 8 | Unchanged — market pain is external |
| Pull | 9 | 9 | Unchanged — but now *experienced* in Week 1, not promised in copy |
| Anxiety | 7 | **4** | Own-number test call at T+15min (trust before money); value-based money-back (risk removed); deterministic path visible in first transcript (malpractice fear addressed); no setup effort (nothing to configure) |
| Habit | 9 | **6** | Instant gratification breaks the "$0 is fine" equilibrium; the trial is priced against the $1,900 leak, not $499; same-day live removes the effort objection |
| **Net** | **17 vs 16 — tie (switch only at struggling moments)** | **17 vs 10 — +7 switch margin** | **Spontaneous switching becomes possible; struggling moments become tipping accelerants instead of prerequisites** |

**Persona B:**

| Force | Before | After SLIP Design | What Changed It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push | 6 | 6 | Unchanged |
| Pull | 8 | 8 | Unchanged |
| Anxiety | 6 | **4** | Ecosystem play (CRM export included in pilot) removes integration risk; case studies + named references still required (Section AG-3) |
| Habit | 8 | **6** | Staff-in-place inertia weakened by the 72-hour proof: first booked consult while existing staff is still learning the new hire's name |
| **Net** | **14 vs 14 — tie** | **14 vs 10 — +4 switch margin** | **Scale-efficiency proof lands faster than the emotional switch costs can counter it** |

### The SLIP × FoP Synthesis

1. **SLIP doesn't create demand — it converts it.** The Push/Pull forces come from the market (Section AB); SLIP's job is to neutralize Anxiety and Habit so the existing demand can flow. The +7/+4 margins above are the mathematical expression of that.
2. **The Habit Flip is the endgame.** Habit is the only force that changes sides: it blocks adoption pre-flip and prevents churn post-flip. Day-60 is the strategic inflection point of the entire customer relationship.
3. **The Day-1 Wow path is testable.** The T+15min ownership moment, the T+24h dinner text, the T+72h booking — each is a discrete hypothesis that the Discovery interview program (Section AA) must validate. If 20–30 interviews confirm the sequence, SLIP's design survives contact with customers; if not, the sequence reorders.
4. **Compliance boundary:** the SLIP redesign never touches claims language. "Apply for Access" stays (Section S); only the process behind it becomes frictionless. The value-based guarantee is worded as "consult you'd have missed," never "case signed" (Section A).

**Bottom line:** TrueVow's strategy was already sound (Sections Y–AI); its adoption mechanics were not. The SLIP redesign compresses value from "within the first month" to "within the first 72 hours," rebalances the buying forces from a 17:16 tie to a 17:10 margin, and converts the biggest blocker (Habit 9/10) into the biggest retention asset (post-flip switching cost) — provided the Day-1 path is built and validated before the beachhead scales.

### AU-4. The Voice Rules — How to Write It Without Pitching

1. **Second person, present tense, concrete scenes.** "It's 2:14 on a Tuesday" — never "PI firms often miss calls."
2. **Numbers are characters.** 38%. $1,900. Two rings. Ninety days. Numbers carry the wound; adjectives don't.
3. **Never say the category words.** No "AI receptionist," "answering service," "virtual receptionist" (AS-6 rule 1). The story's world has phones, weekends, and records — not categories.
4. **No claims about TrueVow. Statements about their day.** "Benjamin answers with your firm name" — not "TrueVow is the leading intake platform."
5. **Dignity, never victimhood.** They are capable professionals bleeding in silence — the story respects the 15 years, never mocks them. Condescension kills the Care arc.
6. **Every claim has a compliance-safe rendering** (AS-3 table). "Never miss a case" internally = "every call answered, every record visible" publicly.
7. **One Ask per surface** — always 15-minute-sized (AS-2).
8. **The story ends where the demo begins.** The narrative's only job is to get them to Beat 4. The product tells the rest.
